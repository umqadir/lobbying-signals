      About the data

      What this is. A tracker of federal lobbying disclosures filed with the Senate under the Lobbying Disclosure Act (LDA). It shows which organizations, topics, agencies, and bills have more or less reported lobbying activity than a year earlier.

      Where the data comes from. Quarterly activity reports from the official Senate LDA database, refreshed daily. Coverage: several years of filings. Every example filing links to the official Senate record.

      What a “mention” is. Each filing describes its lobbying activities in free text. Versioned, deterministic rules (no AI model calls) tag those descriptions with topics, agencies, and legislation. One activity description that matches a tag counts as one mention, so a single filing can produce many mentions. Bill numbers are scoped to their Congress (an “H.R. 1” from 2017 and one from 2025 are different laws), and a law's number, short name, and public-law number are counted as one bill.

      How comparisons work. Every view uses the same two comparison periods. Both are year-over-year and both use report quarters (Q1 = Jan–Mar, Q2 = Apr–Jun, Q3 = Jul–Sep, Q4 = Oct–Dec). LDA reports are filed quarterly and most arrive just before each deadline, so comparing rolling date windows would mostly measure when reports were filed. The default view compares the latest complete report quarter with the same quarter a year earlier; lobbying is seasonal, so same-quarter comparisons are like for like. The second view covers the most recent quarter, whose reports are still arriving: reports filed so far are compared with reports for the same quarter last year that had been filed by the same date. Early in a filing period that sample is small, and filing counts are shown for both sides. “Share” is a tag's percentage of all tagged mentions in a period, which adjusts for changes in total volume.

      Normalized view. The Normalized tickbox restates aggregate dollar figures in constant dollars (CPI-U, base = the latest complete calendar year, each quarter deflated by its own factor) and switches mention histories to each quarter's share of all tagged activity. This removes the effect of overall inflation and of growth in total filing volume. Individual filing records always show as-filed amounts.

      Confidence. A rule-based label based on how much volume sits behind a change and how far its share moved against the year-ago quarter. It is not a statistical test.

      Limitations. These are descriptive comparisons, not causal claims. Income is reported per filing, not per issue, so income figures attribute a filing's whole income to each of its tags. The newest quarter is incomplete until well after the statutory deadline. Rule-based tagging misses paraphrases a person would catch. Amendments and termination reports are included: when a filer amends or terminates a report, its latest filing replaces the original for that report period, so the figures shown are the corrected ones, not the amounts originally filed. A small number of filings report amounts that are not U.S.-dollar lobbying spending (for example, an amount stated in a currency the filer issued itself). These were checked by hand against the Senate record and are excluded; the reasons and evidence are documented in the source code.

      Organization spend. The Organizations view tracks reported dollars rather than mention counts: each client's reported income or expenses, summed per organization per report quarter, under the same two comparison periods as everything else. A quarter counts as complete about six weeks after its statutory filing deadline, so late filings do not appear as a decline. Name variants for the same organization (legal-suffix differences, "on behalf of" filers, former names) are combined. These are quarterly LDA totals and are not divided among the topics an organization lobbied on.
